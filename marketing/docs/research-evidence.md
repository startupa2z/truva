## Published statistic

- Source: [IBM, Cost of a Data Breach Report 2026](https://www.ibm.com/reports/data-breach), describing research with Ponemon Institute.
- Earlier concept value: **USD 4.99 million**, the reported global average cost of a data breach.
- Scope: a breach-cost benchmark. It is not the average cost to repair a software bug, a startup-specific estimate, an expected loss per company, or evidence of a Truva assessment's financial return.
- The figure is separate from the explicitly illustrative lifecycle graphic. No measured values are assigned to the graphic's stages. The source link and year are visible directly beside the statistic.
- IBM's landing page is a rolling URL; reverify the figure and report year before publication and future updates.

## Why not an exponential bug-cost claim?

[Menzies, Nichols, Shull and Layman, “Are Delayed Issues Harder to Resolve?”](https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.04886), published in Empirical Software Engineering (2017), studied 171 software projects from 2006–2014. They found that later resolution did not consistently require substantially greater effort. This does not prove delay is harmless, but it rules out presenting a universal exponential multiplier as settled fact.

[NIST's June 2002 report summary](https://www.nist.gov/system/files/documents/2021/03/24/econImpactSumm.v23.pdf) estimates economy-wide costs of inadequate software testing. Its age, macroeconomic scope and methodology make it unsuitable as a current dollar price per startup bug. It is not used in the homepage statistic.

The research reviewed did not establish a current universal per-bug repair price or a publicly verifiable Gartner multiplier. No statistic is attributed to Gartner. An assessment identifies and prioritizes risks; the current research does not substantiate a promise that an assessment prevents all breaches or saves the cited amount.
